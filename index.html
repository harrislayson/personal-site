01 / Stanford & beyond

Finding my people.
Following the questions.

I’m Harris. I study Symbolic Systems at Stanford, play rugby, and spend a lot of time thinking about AI, people, and how businesses grow.

Before Stanford, home was Winter Park, Florida, and a blended family with four siblings. Debate filled much of high school and left me with an interest in politics and philosophy that I’ve carried into college.

A first year on the West Coast

Making an unfamiliar place feel like home.

Moving to Stanford was my first time living on the West Coast. Rugby and Stanford Consulting quickly became the communities I invested most in. I also joined a Stanford Pre-Business Association cohort, where a business insights program gave me my first exposure to finance.

I put a lot of time into those groups and took on leadership responsibilities as the year went on. They gave me people to learn from, teammates to spend time with, and reasons to get involved beyond the classroom.

The summer after freshman year

A summer with room to think.

I spent it in Lobitos, Peru, volunteering with EcoSwell, a sustainability organization on the Piura coast. I rebuilt how the organization presents itself online and planned programming that can keep running through a bad El Niño season.

I also learned to surf. Being a beginner at something again, every morning, turned out to be the most useful part of the trip.

The rest of it went into conversations: Stanford alumni in banking and consulting, plus dozens of founders and executives I met while sourcing cases for Stanford Consulting. Hearing what those jobs actually feel like day to day is what pointed me toward banking, growth, and consulting.

02 / Open questions

Things I’m still
thinking through.

A few interests I’m following as a student. These are questions I’m exploring, with plenty still to learn.

Transhumanism

Where do we draw the line between a tool and an extension of ourselves?

I’ve become interested in research on transhumanism and the possibility of using technology to expand human capabilities. My interest in philosophy makes the questions around identity, agency, and what we value about being human especially compelling.

I pay just as much attention to the companies pushing the frontier here: the ones actually building neural interfaces, longevity research, and human augmentation rather than writing about them.

Open-weight AI

Who gets to decide what a model can say?

I’m interested in censorship resistance and open-weight AI ecosystems. I want to better understand how the ability to run and adapt models changes access, control, and dependence on the organizations that build them.

There are difficult questions about responsibility alongside that independence. Both sides are worth taking seriously.

Politics

What does an AI race with authoritarian states actually buy them?

The competition between the United States and China over frontier AI is the geopolitical story I follow most closely. Compute, chips, and talent are being treated as strategic assets, and export controls have turned into foreign policy.

The part I keep coming back to is what these systems will let an authoritarian government actually do: surveillance that scales without hiring anyone, censorship that adapts in real time, and information operations that cost almost nothing to run. That feels like a more concrete near-term risk than most of what gets discussed.

03 / Off the clock

What I do with the rest of the week.

A team to come back to

Rugby made Stanford feel smaller.

The rugby team became one of my main communities during freshman year. I’ve loved having that group to train with and spend time with.

I was elected social chair for the ’26–’27 season, which is a real chance to rebuild the team’s culture: how we recruit, how new players get brought in, and what the group feels like off the pitch.

The nine-day tour of Ireland added another place to our shared story: Belfast and Dublin, matches against local clubs, and a game at Trinity, the world’s oldest rugby club.

In the water

Surfing.

I grew up on the water and picked up wake surfing in high school. Learning to actually surf in Peru this summer was the natural next step, and it stuck.

I’m planning to keep at it near Stanford, where the water is a lot colder and the waves are less forgiving.

Trails & detours

Getting to know the West.

As a kid, I camped in North Carolina. At Stanford, I’ve backpacked in the Sierras and taken the chance to explore Yosemite, Big Sur, Mount Tam, and Carmel, with a trip north to Seattle, too.

This year, I’m taking outdoor leadership and survival skills classes to keep building on that interest. I want to get more comfortable spending time outside and helping other people do the same.

02Beyond Colonial DestinyPost-colonial democratic stability · DATASCI 112+

I built and cleaned a roughly 90-nation panel from more than ten international sources, standardized across comparable time windows, then modeled post-colonial democratic stability in Python (RandomForest, KNN, and XGBoost), reading the results through permutation importance and residual analysis.

Structural inheritances explained only 41.6% of the variance, with rule of law the dominant predictor. The unexplained residual was the conclusion: outcomes point more toward agency and external influence than toward inherited conditions.

PythonDataset constructionPredictive modeling
